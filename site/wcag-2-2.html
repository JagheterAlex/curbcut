Readiness check

Your last audit was written against WCAG 2.1.

EN 301 549 V4.1.1 adopts WCAG 2.2 and is scheduled for citation in the Official Journal on 30 November 2026. Six success criteria arrive at levels A and AA. A scanner can check one of them. This page is about the other five.

No install, no account, no terminal. Every result shows two columns: what fails today under V3.2.1, and what the same page looks like once V4.1.1 is cited.

What you will have to do by hand

Automated testing measures geometry, contrast and markup. Five of the six criteria arriving are judgements about behaviour, and nothing in the page source reveals them. Each of these needs a person, sitting with your product:

Criterion Level Who can check it What the check actually is
2.5.8 Target SizeAA A scanner Measure every control against the minimum hit area.
2.4.11 Focus Not ObscuredAA A person Tab through the page and watch whether a sticky header or cookie bar covers the control that just took focus.
2.5.7 Dragging MovementsAA A person Find every drag interaction, then confirm each one can also be done with a single pointer.
3.2.6 Consistent HelpA A person Compare where help appears across several pages, and judge whether it is the same help in the same place.
3.3.7 Redundant EntryA A person Complete a multi-step form and notice anything it asks for twice.
3.3.8 Accessible AuthenticationAA A person Judge whether your login imposes a cognitive function test with no alternative.

This is the part vendors leave out. A tool reporting a clean WCAG 2.2 result has checked one criterion in six and said nothing about the other five. It is not lying about what it found; it is quiet about what it never looked at.

Our scanner prints the five it cannot evaluate, on every result, above the findings. A green result from us reads as a starting point, because that is what it is.

One thing to take off the list

4.1.1 Parsing leaves the standard at citation — and it already cannot be failed. W3C errata of 21 September 2023 added a note to WCAG 2.0 and 2.1 that it “should be considered as always satisfied for any content using HTML or XML”.

So fixing duplicate id attributes buys you no conformance, today or later. If a report has put that on your roadmap, it can come off. The full explanation is in the article.

Before you act on any of it

  • 30 November is a scheduled milestone, not a passed law. An obligation begins when a version is cited in the Official Journal, and scheduled dates move. Until then the harmonised standard is V3.2.1, which adopts WCAG 2.1.
  • Anyone telling you WCAG 2.2 is legally required today is describing next year as though it were this one.
