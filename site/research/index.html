Research

The Curbcut research crawler

If you found this address in your server logs, this page is why. It explains exactly what was requested, how to stop it, and what the data was used for.

What it does

The crawler identifies itself as:

Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; CurbcutResearch/1.0; +https://curbcut.org/research)
  • It requests one page per site: your apex URL. It never follows links and never crawls a second page.
  • It loads that page twice, about a second apart, because a single load of a commercial page is a sample of one.
  • It reads /robots.txt first and does not fetch the page if you disallow it.
  • It runs an accessibility test in a headless browser and keeps the resulting clause list. Your page content is not stored, republished, or passed to anyone.

How to stop it

Add this to your robots.txt and it will skip you on every future run:

User-agent: CurbcutResearch
Disallow: /

You do not need to explain why, and nothing is recorded about you except that you were excluded and the reason was robots.txt. If you would rather write to a person, hello@curbcut.org reaches one.

Study: EU website readiness for EN 301 549 V4.1.1

Run 22 August 2026. The 300 most-visited domains on EU member state TLDs, taken from the Tranco research list (id 74V8X, generated 21 August 2026), measured against the clauses of EN 301 549 that are in force today and the criteria arriving with V4.1.1.

149 of the 300 could be measured. The other 151 are published with the reason each one was excluded, because a study that hides what it discarded is reporting its own rounding.

Download the data (JSON) Read the findings

No site is named

Each row carries a rank bucket and a country TLD, never the domain. The sample can be rebuilt exactly from the Tranco list id and the selection rule, so anyone who wants to check this can reconstruct it and re-run the scan themselves. We decline to publish the list of names, because a vendor that sells the remedy naming the sites turns a measurement into an accusation.

What the data cannot support

  • Automated testing detects roughly 30 to 40 percent of WCAG failures. Every count here is a floor, and a site with no findings has not passed an accessibility audit.
  • One page per site. A conformance claim covers a service; this measured a front door.
  • Failing a criterion is not the same as breaking the law. The harmonised standard gives a presumption of conformance, and missing it removes the presumption rather than establishing a breach.
  • The exclusions are not random. Sites with bot protection blocked the request, and they are plausibly a different kind of organisation from those that did not.
  • Nothing here is legal advice.

Reproducing it

The scanner is MIT licensed and the script that produced this study is in the repository at scripts/benchmark.mjs. Disagreeing with the method is easier when you can run it.
